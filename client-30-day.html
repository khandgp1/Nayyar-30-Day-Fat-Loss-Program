Day 0 — Get set up

Three things today. Then you start.

No food changes yet — today is just tools and picks, so Day 1 has something to stand on.

Task 1 · The scaleBuy a digital kitchen food scale that measures in grams — any brand, under $20. Get it today (in-store or same-day). It's the one tool everything else needs.

Task 2 · Yogurt reconBuy plain 0% Greek yogurt — a few different brands. The only filter: the ingredients read milk + live cultures, nothing else. No added sugar, no "vanilla," no "honey" — those hide 10–15g of sugar. Send a photo of each tub's label.

Task 3 · Pick your proteinsFrom the list below, tell me your top 3 — just the three you'd actually eat. It's not exhaustive; if you eat something lean that's missing, say so.

Tuna / chicken pouch (in water) Pre-cooked chicken pouch Frozen grilled chicken strips Rotisserie chicken Hard-boiled eggs Deli turkey breast Frozen white fish Frozen salmon Lean beef (90/10)

Done when: scale in hand · yogurt labels sent · top 3 picked

Day 1 — Water in, yogurt in

Two easy installs.

Light on purpose — this gives Day 0 room to finish.

Every morning from now onOn waking, drink 2 liters of water — before coffee, before food. Big bottle, first thing. That's the whole task. (No bottle with a steel or glass interior? Grab a good one today.)

Start the yogurtStart eating the yogurt — any amount, a spoonful or half a tub. You bought a few brands: find the one you genuinely like. That's the one we build on.

Day 2 — The evening ritual + your first meat

Tonight, you prep tomorrow's breakfast.

This is where the structure starts doing the work for you.

The evening ritual — tonight, then every nightWeigh one serving of yogurt (serving size is on the tub) into a container, lid on, into the fridge. That's tomorrow's breakfast, already waiting. Your morning becomes: wake → water → the yogurt that's sitting there.

The meat run — buy, cook, eat todayFrom your top 3, pick one. Buy enough for the week, prepare it, and eat it — today. You're in the driver's seat on how it's made: seasoning, sides, your taste. Already-cooked options make this easy. Then send a quick report: a photo, what you picked, how you prepped it, and where you bought it.

Day 3 — Taste check

Does it actually taste good?

A lean meat you only tolerate disappears from your plate by week two — so we fix taste now, before it becomes a problem.

Be honest — "it was fine" counts as a no. Three ways this can go:

  • You liked it — great, keep it. We can still tune the seasoning if you want.
  • Close but not there — let's make it better: seasoning, a sauce, a different cooking method. Small fixes beat starting over.
  • Not working — swap it for another pick, no penalty. (Easiest option on the board if you want it: pre-cooked chicken breast strips — no prep, hard to get wrong.)

If you swap, just repeat the meat run today with the new pick — buy, cook, eat, report. No days lost.

Day 4 — Portion the meat

The ritual grows one step.

One thing worth knowing: meat is mostly water, so grams on the scale isn't grams of protein. You don't need the math — just a portion size.

Tonight, then every nightAfter the yogurt, weigh out one portion of your cooked meat and add it to the fridge — ready to grab tomorrow. Scale tip: put the empty container on, hit tare/zero, then add the food so the scale reads just the meat.

The one strict rule

Your prepped food is always the first food you eat each day. Water first, then the containers, before anything else. Be strict about this one — everything you eat after it is still completely yours.

Day 5 — A vegetable joins the stack

The one food that makes everything after it easier.

High-volume, fills you up, almost no calories — so every later change lands on someone who's already full.

First — a quick checkIs broccoli a hard no? If so, we swap in green beans, cauliflower, or a mixed medley — same frozen steam-in-bag category, same task. No point installing a food you've already decided against.

Then — buy it, learn it, stack itGrab a couple brands of frozen steam-in-bag vegetables. Learn it (it's a microwave and a few minutes), taste it today, and tonight add a serving to the prep — alongside the yogurt and the meat. Three containers now.

Flavor freely

  • Salt, pepper, spices
  • Hot sauce, mustard, lemon
  • Light garlic-herb seasoning
  • Anything near-zero-calorie

Skip these

  • Cheese sauce
  • Butter & cream sauces
  • Sugary marinades / glazes
  • "Mac &" anything
Seasoning yes, sauce no → a cheese-sauce bag can double the calories of the vegetable it coats.

Day 7 — Week one, done

One week in. Your morning is owned.

Genuinely — three foods adopted, a ritual running nightly, and not one thing you love has been banned. Three small moves close the week.

Task 1 · One more bottleTake your daily water count and add one more bottle — anywhere in the day. That's the new number. (Morning 2L is unchanged.)

Task 2 · A start lineNever eat earlier than the time you already start eating. You already do this — now it's just a line. (Water, black coffee, and plain tea are always fine.)

Task 3 · Your first carbFrom your list, we start with one pick. Buy enough for the week, and tonight add a serving to the prep — alongside the yogurt, meat, and vegetable. Four containers now.

The board: start-time → 2L water → prepped food first → your bottles through the day → prep tomorrow tonight.

Weeks 2–4 — It keeps running

Same board, small changes.

The routine you built in week one is the whole engine. From here it just gets fine-tuned — and this is what a day looks like, every day.

Morning
  • Hold your start time — nothing before it
  • 2 liters of water on waking
  • Your prepped containers first: yogurt, protein, vegetable, carb — before anything else
Through the day
  • Your water bottles, spread out
  • After the prepped food, the rest of your eating is still yours
Evening
  • Prep tomorrow's containers — same ritual, every night
What's next

Weeks 2–4 are personalized to you. I'll add one small change at a time — a bit more protein, a portion tweak, a longer morning window, some daily walking — based on how your check-ins go. Nothing to memorize now; you'll get each step when it's your turn. And tell me how you're feeling — that's the most useful thing you can report.

After — When the push ends

The program doesn't leave you on its hardest week.

When the final push is done, you go back to exactly the routine you ran before it — same containers, same rituals, same rhythm. Nothing new to learn; you already lived it.

Heads up

Expect the scale to tick up a couple of pounds that week. That's water coming back with your carbs, not fat. It's completely expected and completely fine — don't let it rattle you.
