00 — How to run this document

Two lanes. One rule about which lane he sees.

Every task below is split into what goes out and what stays here.

Send to client

Wire-ready copy. Paraphrase freely to match your chat voice, but the content is fixed — every bolded constraint in a send block is load-bearing.

Coach-side · never disclosed

Done bars, gates, and the reason each rung exists. None of it is a secret because it's embarrassing — it's undisclosed because disclosure invites gaming (the Day 6 interview) or turns a habit into a lecture (the morning water).

The standing doctrineFour principles run under every rung. When improvising, check the improvisation against these:

1 · Ownership is adherenceThe client picks, the menu pre-screens. A food he chose survives week two; a food he was assigned doesn't. Any time you can hand him the choice inside a screened list, do.

2 · Swap, don't forceAny food that stalls gets swapped within its category, at any point, without resetting anything. Pick changes; method never does. A swap is signal, not failure.

3 · Addition before subtractionWeek one bans nothing. The program occupies territory by addition, ordering, and formalizing his own habits back at him. Subtraction only lands on a client who is already full.

4 · Numbers stay coach-sideCalories, deficits, the word "PSMF," the fasting endgame — none of it is disclosed. He receives food-level instructions only.

Day 0 — Tooling & inventory

Three tasks. All completable today. Then Day 1.

Day-zero client, paid, no warm-up phase. Presumption: task completion is taken seriously — every task is one-day-sized and evidence-backed where it matters.

Task 1 · The food scale — today, immediacy first
Send to client
  • Digital kitchen food scale — not a bathroom scale
  • Measures in grams
  • Any brand, under $20
  • Get it today. In-store or same-day delivery. If a delivery option says Thursday, go to a store instead.
Coach-side
Done bar
Scale in hand today. No evidence required — it proves itself the first time he logs food.
Why
The scale is the gating tool for everything after Day 0. No scale = the program can't start. That's why immediacy outranks brand, price, and features.
Task 2 · Yogurt recon — today or tomorrow
Send to client
  • Buy plain 0% Greek yogurt — several different brands.
  • The filter: the ingredient list must read milk + live cultures only. No added sugar, no "vanilla," no "honey" — those are imposters hiding 10–15g of sugar per serving.
  • Send a photo of each tub, including its ingredient label. Every brand you buy — tub + label.
Coach-side
Done bar
Photos received, every label passes the filter.
Why
The label photo is the point, not the tub — it proves he executed the filter and trains label-reading as a day-zero reflex. Multiple brands now means he locks the one he genuinely likes later; adherence lives in the brand he'd eat anyway.
If it fails
A failed label carries no penalty — flag it, he swaps it on the next trip. The reflex is what's being installed.
Task 3 · Pick your proteins — today, just a reply
Send to client

The menu — make clear it is not exhaustive; if he eats something lean that isn't listed, he says so. Ask: pick your top 3. No ranking, no commitment — just the three he'd actually eat.

Tuna / chicken pouch or can, in water Pre-cooked chicken pouch Frozen grilled chicken strips Rotisserie chicken (skin off) Pre-peeled hard-boiled eggs Sealed deli turkey breast Frozen white fish Frozen salmon portions Lean beef ≥90/10
Coach-side
Done bar
Three picks received.
Why
The pick is the client's — ownership of the choice is where adherence lives. The menu just guarantees every option is one a deficit can be built on.
What it loads
The three picks are the working inventory: one becomes the anchor protein, the others are swaps when it stalls. Nothing is bought yet — buying comes with the first food task.
Deliberately omitted from Day 0

Starting weight — not captured. Starting the process outranks proving anything to the coach or to himself. Revisit if/when a measurement becomes useful to the program (see Lever 8) — not a Day 0 gate.

Any food change. Nothing about his eating changes today. Day 0 is tooling and inventory only.

Exit when all three clear: scale in hand · yogurt photos in, labels pass · top-3 picks received → Day 1

Day 1 — Water, questions, breathing room

Light by design. One new install.

Day 0 tasks get room to finish. Day 1 is the open-floor day.

Task 1 · Morning water — starts tomorrow morning, runs daily forever
Send to client
  • On waking, drink 2 liters of water — before coffee, before food. Big bottle, first thing. That's the whole task.
  • Bottle check: do you have a regular-sized water bottle? If not, buy one today — a good one with a non-plastic interior (stainless steel or glass).
Coach-side
Standing
Permanent habit from Day 1 forward — it never retires.
Why
Not disclosed as a lecture: it blunts morning appetite, displaces caloric morning drinks, and reclassifies a lot of "hunger" as thirst. First lever that quietly reduces intake without a single food rule.
Note
His bottle size becomes known here — Day 6's water audit does its math with it (bottles × size).
Task 2 · Start eating the yogurt — today
Send to client
  • Start eating the yogurt today. Any amount — a spoonful or half a tub, doesn't matter.
  • You bought several brands: find the one you like best. That's the one we build on.
Coach-side
Why
No serving size, no timing, no rules — pure adoption. The only output that matters is a brand preference, because adherence lives in the one he'd eat anyway.

Also on Day 1Answer his questions. Whatever came up from Day 0 — brands, picks, logistics — handle it in chat. No other installs. If Day 0 tasks are still in flight, today is the buffer to close them out.

Exit when: Day 0 fully cleared · water habit acknowledged → Day 2

Day 2 — The yogurt ritual + the meat run

Two installs, both food. Displacement begins.

This is the day the program starts displacing calories with structure. Nothing is restricted yet — the new foods start crowding out what they replace.

Carrying forward — reinforce, don't re-teach

Morning water continues. Emphasize: the slight excess is critical — drinking a bit more than feels necessary is the point, not a side effect. Don't let it quietly shrink to a comfortable glass.

Keep eating the yogurt, any amount, any time. The free-form eating from Day 1 doesn't stop just because tonight's ritual starts.

Task 1 · The evening ritual — tonight, then every night
Send to client
  • Tonight, and every night from now on: weigh out one serving of yogurt on your new scale, in grams. The serving size is on the container — read it there.
  • Lid on, into the fridge. That's tomorrow's breakfast, already waiting.
  • Morning stack from here: wake → 2L water → the yogurt that's sitting there.
Coach-side
Standing
Permanent ritual — evening prep, morning eat, every day.
Why
The scale's first daily rep, in a zero-pressure context — weighing a food he already bought, not policing a meal. Quietly installs a fixed breakfast: the first meal the program now owns, with zero morning friction and zero morning decisions.
Task 2 · The meat run — today: buy, cook, eat
Send to client
  • From your top 3: pick one, buy enough for the week, prepare it, eat it — today. Already-cooked or minimal-prep options are better.
  • You're in the driver's seat on how it's served — seasoning, sides, prep style, your taste. Want to talk it through first? I'm here; happy to throw suggestions.
  • Then send the full report: photo of what you made · what you picked · ingredients and/or label if one exists · how you prepped it · where you bought it · anything else relevant.
Coach-side
Done bar
Photo + report received, meat eaten at least once today.
Why
Day 2 is selection and starting — no quantity floor, no portion rules, no frequency target yet. Adoption first. The driver's-seat framing is the point: adherence lives in a preparation he chose. Coach's role today is consult-on-request, not prescription.
The report
Richest evidence pull yet — sourcing, label literacy, and prep style in one artifact. Everything in it is calibration data for the cutting phase.

Exit when: tonight's yogurt serving prepped · meat bought, cooked, eaten, report in → Day 3

Day 3 — The taste checkpoint

Quality control on the anchor protein.

No new install. A lean meat he merely tolerates disappears from his plate by week two — today is a conversation day with one conditional task.

The check-in · coach opens, in chat — three questions, in order
Ask the client
  1. "Did you like it?" Honest answer — "it was fine" counts as a no worth probing.
  2. Close but not there? "Let's make it taste better." Work the preparation, not the pick: seasoning, sauce, sides, cooking method. He drives, you suggest. Small fixes here are cheaper than a swap.
  3. Not working? "Do you want to swap and try something else?" No penalty, no sunk-cost pressure. Advice when asked: pre-cooked chicken breast strips are the easiest option on the board — no prep, no skill, hard to get wrong.
Conditional task · the swap — only if he swaps
Send to client

Today: go buy the new pick and repeat the Day 2 task in full — buy enough for the week, prepare it, eat it, and send the same full report (photo, pick, label, prep, source, anything relevant).

Coach-side
Timeline
The swap repeats the task, not the timeline — no days lost, the program doesn't pause. Swap-and-report happens same-day.
Rule
Swap the pick, never the method. If the second pick stalls too, same checkpoint again — the menu is deep enough to survive several misses.
Read
A swap is signal, not failure: every rejected pick sharpens the picture of what he'll actually eat — the only picture that matters.

Standing habits by end of Day 31 · 2L water on waking, before coffee, before food  2 · Yogurt daily, any amount + evening ritual  3 · A week of lean protein in the house, in a preparation he chose — or a same-day replacement already bought and reported.

Exit when: taste verdict delivered — kept (± prep upgrades) or swapped (new meat bought, eaten, reported same-day) → Day 4

Day 4 — The ritual grows: portion the meat

One addition, one teach, one strict rule.

Stacked on an existing habit — and the first teach: grams of food is not grams of protein, and he almost certainly doesn't know that yet. Teach it once, simply, tonight.

Task · add meat portioning to the evening ritual — tonight, then every night
Send to client
  • The evening ritual grows one step. After the yogurt: weigh out one portion of your cooked meat — enough for 20–40 grams of protein — container, lid, fridge. Ready to grab tomorrow, same as the yogurt.
  • Scale tip: empty container on the scale, hit tare/zero, then add the meat. The scale now reads just the food.
  • The intent behind the prep, so it's clear: the prepped food is always the first food you eat in the day. Water first, then the prepped containers, before anything else. Be strict about this one — nothing else gets eaten before it.
Coach-side
Done bar
Tonight's ritual produces two containers in the fridge — yogurt and a meat portion. On his word; no photo needed for a habit extension.
Milestone
First time the scale measures a target rather than just recording. He's producing portions to a spec — the core mechanic every later cutting task runs on.
The rule
The eat-first rule is the program's first strict rule and first true displacement. Everything before was addition; this forces prepped food to the front of the day, on top of 2L of water — most of the morning's old intake gets crowded out without banning a single food. Keep the asymmetry: what he eats first is rigid; everything after is untouched.
The teach · food grams vs protein grams — walk him through it in chat
Tell the client — one idea only

Meat is mostly water — the protein is only part of the weight. He is not learning macros; he's learning one conversion for one food.

If his pick has a label (pouches, deli packs, frozen strips): use it. Find protein-per-serving, do the simple multiple with him once in chat — one pouch says 20g protein, so one pouch is a portion, done.

If his pick has no label (rotisserie, home-cooked): the rule of thumb, not a spreadsheet →

Exit when: teach landed (he states his one number back) · ritual ran with both containers → Day 5

Day 5 — Broccoli enters the stack

The satiety lever that makes every later subtraction cheaper.

One install: high-volume, high-fiber vegetable. Frozen steam-in-bag — keeps for weeks, microwaves in minutes, cheap, zero cooking skill.

The opener · ask FIRST, before the task goes out
Ask the client

"Is broccoli a hard no for you?"

Coach-side
If no-go
Adjust the pick — green beans, cauliflower, mixed medley. Same frozen-bag category, same task. Don't install a food he's already decided against; adherence lives in a vegetable he'll actually eat.
Task · buy it, learn it, sample it, stack it — today
Send to client
  • Buy frozen broccoli — the steam-in-bag kind, a couple of different brands. Pre-seasoned is fine if the calorie impact is minimal — light garlic-herb passes, broccoli mac & cheese obviously doesn't. Common sense: seasoning yes, sauce meal no. Check the label if unsure.
  • Learn to cook it today. It's a microwave and a few minutes — read the bag, run a batch, see what it needs (salt, seasoning, whatever makes it work — driver's seat, same as the meat).
  • Sample some today.
  • Tonight the ritual grows again: add a serving of broccoli (serving size on the bag) alongside the yogurt and the meat portion.
  • This stacks on everything else. Nothing retires — water, yogurt, meat, eat-first rule all still running. The prepped food just got one container bigger.
Coach-side
Done bar
Bought, sampled, and tonight's ritual produced three containers. On his word.
Why
The morning stack is now water → yogurt → meat → broccoli before any old food fires. That's most of a stomach spoken for. Every subtraction from here lands on a client who is already full.
Checkpoint
Taste checkpoint applies here too: if broccoli stalls in a few days, swap within the frozen-bag category — pick changes, method never does.

Seasoning · passes

  • Salt, pepper, spices
  • Hot sauce, lemon
  • Light garlic-herb bags
  • Near-zero-calorie flavor — unrestricted, he drives it

Sauce · fails

  • Cheese sauce
  • Butter sauce
  • "Mac &" anything
  • Any sauce-based variant
Base plan's toppings-unrestricted logic does not port here → a cheese-sauce bag can double the calories of the vegetable it coats, and 30 days can't absorb that.

Exit when: hard-no check cleared (or pick adjusted) · bought, sampled, in tonight's ritual → Day 6

Day 6 — Catch-up + the interview

Consolidate — and quietly load the next phase.

No new install. Five days of stacking gets a consolidation day. Meanwhile the coach interviews: every question below is loading a future rung, and none of that is disclosed.

The day itselfAdjust and/or catch up. Any Day 0–5 task still open closes today. Any friction — a meat that's stalling, a ritual step that keeps slipping — gets talked through and fixed. Nothing new lands.

Interview Q1 · Eating window
Ask the client

"So far, about what time is the earliest you start eating? And the latest?"

Coach-side
Loads
Maps his current eating window. The eat-first rule already owns the front; the answer tells you where the back of the window sits — raw material for the future rung that trims late eating (Lever 5).
Never say
Do not telegraph that late eating may get trimmed — that invites pre-emptive gaming of the answer. Both ends, asked neutrally, no judgment attached.

Coach-side
Loads
Inventory for the carb install, whenever that rung fires (Day 7, Task 3). Same pattern as the protein menu: pre-screened list, his picks, ownership stays with him. No purchase, no task — preferences only.
Interview Q3 · Water audit
Ask the client

"Accurate number, not the ideal one: how many bottles of water are you actually getting through in a day, morning included?"

Coach-side
Loads
Verifies the 2L habit is really running and calibrates total daily intake. The accurate-not-ideal framing matters — you're asking for data, not a performance. Bottle size is known from Day 1, so bottles × size does the math. Feeds Day 7, Task 1.
Interview notes

Three questions, one sitting, kept conversational — this is the program's first data pull since Day 0, and the first one measuring him rather than his shopping. Nothing revealed about why each question is asked. Log the answers coach-side — they are the load-in for the next phase: carbs (what), eating window (when), water (whether the foundation holds).

Exit when: all Day 0–5 tasks closed · three interview answers logged → Day 7

Day 7 — Week one closes

Congratulations, then three moves.

Day 6's interview answers get converted into rungs the day after they were gathered. Every task below is personalized off his own data: his water count, his eating time, his carb picks.

Open with the congratulations — genuine, brief
Coach-side
Shape
One week in: morning owned, three foods adopted, ritual running nightly. Say it plainly — the program's first milestone acknowledgment. Then move; don't linger into ceremony.
Task 1 · One more bottle
Send to client

Coach-side

Never say

Compose

LEVER 1Protein up

Coach-side
Routes
Two: bigger portions of current foods (the Day 4 number moves up), or new options, week-one style — pick from his top 3 / the menu, buy, adopt, portion into the ritual. Same pattern, no new teaching needed.
Direction
Protein becomes the backbone of every eating day. Also quiet PSMF prep — the higher his comfortable protein baseline, the cheaper a PSMF day is later (Lever 7).

LEVER 2Carb math — coach-side

Coach-side
Compute
How many healthy-carb servings bring him to a good caloric range — enough to make the day satisfying and sustainable, sitting inside a deficit.
Never say
He experiences this as "add another rice cup at dinner," never as arithmetic. All math stays coach-side — the base plan's never-reveal-the-numbers rule ports to this timeline intact.

LEVER 3Calories down

Coach-side
Gate
The plate is built — Levers 1–2 running.
Method
Incremental decreases to deepen the deficit. Carbs are the dial — protein and vegetables don't get cut. Increments small, one at a time, each held long enough to read his response before the next.
Never say
He hears "we're trimming the rice to one cup" — never a calorie number.

LEVER 4The check-in — standing, throughout

Tell the client — directly

Regular pulse on adherence, issues, and how he's feeling. Tell him: his feelings are important data. The process — and the coach's job — is to make all of this effortless for him, as it should be. Friction reported = the program adjusts, not the client toughing it out.

Coach-side
Function
The check-in is the gate on every other lever. Nothing escalates onto a client who's straining. Feelings are the instrument panel for Levers 3, 5, 6, and 7.

LEVER 5Push the floor back — toward regular fasting

Coach-side
Method
The Day 7 floor moves back in 2-hour increments — one push at a time, each held until it feels like nothing, then the next. Slowly walks him into regular fasting without ever naming a protocol.
Tell him
Stay caffeinated through the window: coffee, tea, sparkling water — all fine before the floor, all appetite management.
Compose
Every push shortens the eating window and drags the eat-first stack later into the day — the prepped food starts owning an even larger share of total intake, for free.

LEVER 6Steps — only when nutrition is running

Coach-side
Gate
The nutritional elements are installed and stable. Then, and only then: 10–15k steps per day.
Why walking
The program's only movement install — consistent with the base ethos: no gym in 30 days; walking is the deficit lever with near-zero adherence cost and no skill requirement.

LEVER 7PSMF tests → final week

Coach-side
Test day
Occasional single-day PSMF tests: eat only the prepped protein + vegetables — the carb sits out for the day. The ritual has been quietly assembling the PSMF food set since Day 5; a test day is subtraction from an existing routine, not a new protocol.
Sequence
1-day test → read response → 2-day test → read response. Response = adherence held, feelings steady at check-in, no rebound blowout after.
Earn it
The final week is a PSMF — if he's responsive. Earned by test results, not scheduled regardless. If tests showed strain, the final week stays a regular deficit — and that is a fine outcome.

LEVER 8The weigh-in ask — soft, early in Phase 2

Pitch shape

"One number a week helps me steer this for you. Same conditions every time — morning, after bathroom, before food or water. You never have to like the number; it's for me, not a scorecard."

Coach-side
Framing
An ask, not a mandate — Day 0 deliberately skipped the scale, and that grace doesn't get revoked by decree.
If yes
Log weekly, same conditions each time. Becomes the primary responsiveness read for Levers 3 and 7 — calorie trims and PSMF earn/hold decisions.
If no
Don't push. Steer on secondary instruments — adherence, check-in feel, clothes fitting, energy. The program survives without the scale; it's just steering with one less gauge.

LEVER 9The simplification — "from now on, life is simple"

Coach-side · gate first
Gate
Fires only once the plate is fully built — Levers 1–2 running, protein backbone + calculated carbs feeding him properly. You cannot ban food outside the plan until the plan is feeding him enough; before that, this rule just creates hunger with nowhere to go.
Send to client — plainly

"From now on, life is simple. No alcohol, no drinks with calories, no dessert, no food outside our plan. And here's the fun part: go find every zero-calorie soda and drink you like — those are unlimited. Enjoy them."

Coach-side
What it is
The program's total-lockdown moment — the black box of untracked eating closes in one move. Everything before was addition and ordering; this single decree converts the plan from "part of his day" to his entire intake.
The hunt
The zero-calorie hunt is load-bearing, not a consolation: an active, immediate yes alongside the no, keeps the flavor habit's slot filled, and mirrors the buy-several-brands pattern he already knows from yogurt and broccoli.
Payoff
Once this fires, Levers 2 and 3 get vastly sharper — with no black box, coach-side calorie math is close to exact.

RULESPhase 2 operating rules

1 · One lever per adjustmentNever two changes at once — otherwise a bad read can't be attributed.

2 · The check-in gates everythingA strained client gets consolidation, not escalation. Week one's install-install-consolidate cadence continues; consolidation days are still real days.

3 · Numbers stay coach-sideCalories, deficits, the word "PSMF," the fasting endgame of the floor pushes — none of it is disclosed. He receives food-level instructions only.

4 · Protein and vegetables are never the cutCarbs are the calorie dial; the ritual's protein core only ever grows or holds.

5 · Swap-don't-force survives all phasesAny food that stalls gets swapped within its category, at any point, without resetting anything.

Day 31+ — The exit ramp

The program does not end on its most aggressive protocol.

After the PSMF — whether the final week or the last test — the client comes back to the previous routine: the built plate he ran before the PSMF began.

End of Day 30 / end of the PSMF
Send to client

"The push is done. We go back to exactly what you were doing before this week — same ritual, same containers, same floor time, same drinks rules. Nothing new to learn; you already lived it."

Coach-side
The return
To the pre-PSMF deficit routine, not to his old life — ritual, eat-first, floor time, simplification rules all keep running. The PSMF was a temporary deepening inside a structure that never stopped.
After
What happens beyond Day 31+ — maintenance, a second cut, transition into the long-game base plan — is a separate conversation, held then.
Warn the bounce — before it happens

Expect the scale to bounce a few lbs as carbs and their water return. Tell him in plain terms, in advance: "the scale will tick up a couple pounds this week — that's water coming back with the carbs, not fat. It's expected and it's fine." An unwarned bounce is how clients torch a real result.
