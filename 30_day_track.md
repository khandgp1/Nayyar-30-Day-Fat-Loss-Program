# 30-Day Fat Loss Track — Day 0

> Day-zero client, paid, no GM phase. Presumption: task completion is taken
> seriously. Every task today is completable in one day and evidence-backed
> where it matters. Day 0 is done when all three tasks clear.

---

## Task 1 — Buy a food scale (today, prioritize immediacy)

**Spec (send to client):**

- Digital **kitchen food scale** — not a bathroom scale
- Measures in **grams**
- Any brand, **under $20**
- **Get it today.** In-store or same-day delivery. If a delivery option says
  Thursday, go to a store instead.

**Coach notes:**

- The scale is the gating tool for everything after Day 0. No scale = the
  program can't start. This is why immediacy outranks brand, price, features.
- No evidence required — the scale proves itself the first time he logs food.

---

## Task 2 — Yogurt recon (today or tomorrow)

**Send to client:**

- Buy **plain 0% Greek yogurt** — several different brands.
- The filter: the ingredient list must read **milk + live cultures only.**
  No added sugar, no "vanilla," no "honey" — those are imposters hiding
  10–15g of sugar per serving.
- **Send a photo of each tub, including its ingredient label.** Every brand
  you buy, tub + label.

**Coach notes:**

- **Done bar:** photos received, every label passes the filter.
- The label photo is the point, not the tub. It proves he executed the actual
  filter and trains label-reading as a day-zero reflex.
- Multiple brands now = he locks the one he genuinely likes later. Adherence
  lives in the brand he'd eat anyway.
- If a label fails the filter, no penalty — flag it, he swaps it on the next
  trip. The reflex is what's being installed.

## Task 3 — Pick your proteins (today, just a reply)

**Send to client — the menu (make clear it is NOT exhaustive; if he eats
something lean that isn't listed, he says so):**

- **Tuna or chicken in a pouch/can, in water** — shelf-stable, rip-and-eat
- **Pre-cooked chicken pouch** — plain, in water, open-and-eat, no fish
- **Frozen grilled chicken strips** — microwave 60–90s or eat cold
- **Rotisserie chicken** — pull the skin; 3–4 day fridge life
- **Pre-peeled hard-boiled eggs** — open-and-eat, keeps about a week
- **Sealed deli turkey breast** — the shelf packs, not the counter; lowest
  sodium available
- **Frozen white fish** (cod / tilapia / pollock) — keeps for weeks, minutes
  to cook
- **Frozen salmon portions** — individually frozen fillets, minimal prep
- **Lean beef** — ≥90/10 ground or a lean cut

**Ask:** *pick your top 3.* No ranking, no commitment — just the three he'd
actually eat.

**Coach notes:**

- **Done bar:** three picks received.
- The pick is the client's — ownership of the choice is where adherence lives.
  The menu just guarantees every option is one a deficit can be built on.
- The three picks are the working inventory: one becomes the anchor protein,
  the others are swaps when it stalls. Nothing is bought yet — buying comes
  with the first food task.

# Day 1 — Water, questions, breathing room

> Light by design. Day 0 tasks get room to finish. One new install.

## Task 1 — morning water (starts tomorrow morning, runs daily from here)

**Send to client:**

- On waking, drink **2 liters of water** — **before coffee, before food.**
- Big bottle, first thing. That's the whole task.
- **Bottle check: do you have a regular-sized water bottle?** If not, buy
  one today — a good one with a **non-plastic interior** (stainless steel
  or glass).

**Coach notes:**

- Permanent standing habit from Day 1 forward — it never retires.
- What it's doing (not disclosed as a lecture): blunts morning appetite,
  displaces caloric morning drinks, and reclassifies a lot of "hunger" as
  thirst. First lever that quietly reduces intake without a single food rule.

## Task 2 — start eating the yogurt (today)

**Send to client:**

- Start eating the yogurt today. **Any amount** — a spoonful or half a tub,
  doesn't matter.
- You bought several brands: **find the one you like best.** That's the one
  we build on.

**Coach notes:**

- No serving size, no timing, no rules — pure adoption. The only output that
  matters is a brand preference, because adherence lives in the one he'd eat
  anyway.

## Also on Day 1

- **Answer the client's questions.** Day 1 is the open-floor day — whatever
  came up from Day 0 (brands, picks, logistics), handle it in chat.
- **No other installs.** If Day 0 tasks are still in flight, today is the
  buffer to close them out.

## Day 1 exit condition

- Day 0 fully cleared, water habit acknowledged. Then Day 2.

# Day 2 — The yogurt ritual + the meat run

> Two installs, both food. This is the day the program starts displacing
> calories with structure. Nothing is restricted yet — the new foods start
> crowding out what they replace.

## Carrying forward (reinforce, don't re-teach)

- **Morning water continues.** Emphasize to the client: **the slight excess is
  critical.** Drinking a bit more than feels necessary is the point, not a
  side effect — don't let it quietly shrink to a comfortable glass.
- **Keep eating the yogurt. Any amount**, any time — the free-form eating from
  Day 1 doesn't stop just because the ritual below starts.

## Task 1 — evening yogurt ritual (tonight, then every night)

**Send to client:**

- Tonight, and every night from now on: **weigh out one serving of yogurt on
  your new scale, in grams.** The serving size is on the container — read it
  there.
- Lid on, into the fridge. **That's tomorrow's breakfast, already waiting.**
- Morning stack from here: wake → 2L water → the yogurt that's sitting there.

**Coach notes:**

- Permanent standing ritual — evening prep, morning eat, every day.
- This is the scale's first daily rep, in a zero-pressure context — weighing
  a food he already bought, not policing a meal.
- Quietly installs a fixed breakfast: the first meal of his day the program
  now owns, with zero morning friction and zero morning decisions.

## Task 2 — the meat run (today: buy, cook, eat)

**Send to client:**

- From your top 3: **pick one, go buy enough for the week, prepare it, eat
  it — today.** Already-cooked or minimal-prep options are better.
- **You're in the driver's seat** on how it's served — seasoning, sides,
  prep style, your taste. If you want to talk it through first, I'm here;
  happy to throw suggestions.
- **Then send the full report:**
  - Photo of what you made
  - What you picked
  - Ingredients and/or the label, if one exists
  - How you prepped it
  - Where you bought it
  - Anything else you think is relevant

**Coach notes:**

- **Done bar:** photo + report received, meat eaten at least once today.
- Day 2 is **selection and starting** — no quantity floor, no portion rules,
  no frequency target yet. Adoption first.
- The driver's-seat framing is the point: adherence lives in a preparation
  he chose. The coach's role today is consult-on-request, not prescription.
- The report is the richest evidence pull yet — it hands over sourcing,
  label literacy, and prep style in one artifact. Everything in it is
  calibration data for the cutting phase.

## Day 2 exit condition

- Yogurt ritual started (tonight's serving prepped)
- Meat bought, cooked, eaten, report received

Then Day 3.

# Day 3 — The taste checkpoint

> No new install today. Day 3 is quality control on the anchor protein —
> because a lean meat he tolerates is a lean meat that disappears from his
> plate by week two. This is a conversation day with one conditional task.

## The check-in (coach opens, in chat)

Three questions, in order:

1. **Did you like it?** Honest answer — "it was fine" counts as a no worth
   probing.
2. **If it's close but not there — let's make it taste better.** Work the
   preparation, not the pick: seasoning, sauce, sides, cooking method. The
   client drives, the coach suggests. Small fixes here are cheaper than a
   swap.
3. **If it's not working — do you want to swap and try something else?**
   No penalty, no sunk-cost pressure. **Coach's advice when asked: pre-cooked
   chicken breast strips are the easiest option on the board** — no prep, no
   skill, hard to get wrong.

## Conditional task — the swap (only if he swaps)

**Send to client:**

- Today: **go buy the new pick and repeat the Day 2 task in full** — buy
  enough for the week, prepare it, eat it, and send the same full report
  (photo, pick, label, prep, source, anything relevant).

**Coach notes:**

- The swap repeats the *task*, not the timeline — no days lost, the program
  doesn't pause. Swap-and-report happens same-day.
- Swap the pick, never the method. If the second pick stalls too, same
  checkpoint again — the menu is deep enough to survive several misses.
- A swap is signal, not failure: every rejected pick sharpens the picture of
  what he'll actually eat, which is the only picture that matters.

## Standing habits by end of Day 3

1. 2L water on waking, before coffee, before food
2. Yogurt daily, any amount + evening ritual (weigh tomorrow's breakfast)
3. A week of lean protein in the house, in a preparation he chose — or a
   same-day replacement already bought and reported

## Day 3 exit condition

- Taste verdict delivered: **kept** (with or without prep upgrades) or
  **swapped** (new meat bought, eaten, reported same-day)

Then Day 4.

# Day 4 — The ritual grows: portion the meat

> One addition, stacked on an existing habit. The evening ritual (yogurt for
> tomorrow) now also portions the cooked meat. This is also the first teach:
> grams of food is not grams of protein, and the client almost certainly
> doesn't know that yet. Teach it once, simply, tonight.

## Task — add meat portioning to the evening ritual (tonight, then every night)

**Send to client:**

- The evening ritual grows one step. After the yogurt: **weigh out one portion
  of your cooked meat — enough for 20–40 grams of protein** — container, lid,
  fridge. It's ready to grab tomorrow, same as the yogurt.
- Scale tip: put the empty container on the scale, hit the **tare/zero**
  button, then add the meat. The scale now reads just the food.
- **Here's the intent behind the prep, so it's clear: the prepped food is
  always the first food you eat in the day.** Water first, then the prepped
  containers, before anything else. **Be strict about this one** — nothing
  else gets eaten before it.

## The teach — food grams vs protein grams (coach walks him through it, in chat)

Keep it to one idea: **meat is mostly water — the protein is only part of the
weight.** He is not learning macros; he's learning one conversion for one food.

**If his pick has a label** (pouches, deli packs, frozen strips): use it.
Find protein-per-serving on the label, do the simple multiple with him once
in chat — e.g. one pouch says 20g protein, so one pouch is a portion, done.

**If his pick has no label** (rotisserie, home-cooked chicken/fish/beef):
give him the rule of thumb, not a spreadsheet —

> **Cooked lean meat is roughly 25–30% protein.** So take the protein you
> want and multiply by ~3.5 to get the meat weight.
> **30g protein ≈ 100–120g of cooked meat on the scale.** That's the number.

**Per-pick anchors (coach-side reference — hand over only the one that
matches his pick):**

| Pick | One portion (20–40g protein) |
|---|---|
| Cooked chicken / turkey breast, rotisserie (skin off) | **70–140g** on the scale |
| Cooked white fish (cod/tilapia/pollock) | **90–170g** |
| Cooked salmon | **80–160g** |
| Cooked ≥90/10 ground beef | **80–150g** |
| Tuna/chicken pouch or can | usually **1 pouch/can** — check the label |
| Frozen grilled strips / deli turkey | **use the label**, target 20–40g protein |
| Hard-boiled eggs | **4–6 eggs** — count, don't weigh (~6g each) |

**Coach notes:**

- **Done bar:** tonight's ritual produces two containers in the fridge —
  yogurt and a meat portion. On his word; no photo needed for a habit
  extension.
- Give him **one number for his one pick.** The table is coach-side. A client
  handed seven conversion rates learns none of them.
- The 20–40g range is the starting band — **once his meat pick is locked,
  the coach pins a precise number** for that specific food and the band
  retires. Practice first, precision once the variable is known.
- Quiet milestone: this is the first time the scale measures a *target*
  rather than just recording. He's now producing portions to a spec —
  the core mechanic every later cutting task runs on.
- **The eat-first rule is the program's first strict rule and first true
  displacement.** Until now everything was addition; this forces the
  prepped food to the front of the day, ahead of whatever used to be
  eaten first. It also front-loads protein on top of 2L of water — most
  of the morning's old intake gets crowded out without a single food
  being banned. Note the asymmetry and keep it: *what* he eats first is
  rigid, everything after is still untouched.

## Day 4 exit condition

- The teach delivered and landed (he can state his one number back)
- Evening ritual ran with both containers

# Day 5 — Broccoli enters the stack

> One install: high-volume, high-fiber vegetable. Frozen steam-in-bag broccoli
> — keeps for weeks, microwaves in minutes, cheap, zero cooking skill. The
> satiety lever that makes every later subtraction cheaper.

## The opener (coach asks FIRST, before assigning)

- **"Is broccoli a hard no for you?"** — asked before the task goes out. If
  it's a genuine no, adjust the pick (green beans, cauliflower, mixed medley —
  same frozen-bag category, same task). Don't install a food he's already
  decided against; adherence lives in a vegetable he'll actually eat.

## Task — buy it, learn it, sample it, stack it (today)

**Send to client:**

- Go buy **frozen broccoli** — the steam-in-bag kind. Grab a couple of
  different brands. **Pre-seasoned is fine if the calorie impact is minimal**
  — a light garlic-herb seasoning passes, broccoli mac & cheese obviously
  doesn't. Common sense: seasoning yes, sauce meal no. Check the label if
  unsure.
- **Learn to cook it today.** It's a microwave and a few minutes — read the
  bag, run a batch, see what it needs (salt, seasoning, whatever makes it
  work for you — driver's seat, same as the meat).
- **Sample some today.**
- Tonight, the evening ritual grows again: **add a serving of broccoli**
  (serving size is on the bag) to the prep — alongside the yogurt and the
  meat portion.
- **This stacks on everything else.** Nothing retires: water on waking,
  yogurt, meat portion, eat-first rule — all still running. The prepped food
  just got one container bigger.

**Coach notes:**

- **Done bar:** bought, sampled, and tonight's ritual produced three
  containers. On his word.
- **Seasoning yes, sauce no.** Pre-seasoned bags pass if the calorie impact
  is minimal; sauce-based variants (cheese, butter sauce, "mac &" anything)
  fail. The base plan's toppings-unrestricted logic doesn't port to this
  timeline — a cheese-sauce bag can double the calories of the vegetable it
  coats, and 30 days can't absorb that. He also still drives seasoning
  himself: salt, pepper, spices, hot sauce, lemon — near-zero-calorie flavor
  is unrestricted.
- The morning stack is now water → yogurt → meat → broccoli before any old
  food fires. That's most of a stomach spoken for. Every subtraction from
  here lands on a client who is already full.
- Taste checkpoint applies here too: if broccoli stalls in a few days, swap
  within the frozen-bag category — pick changes, method never does.

## Day 5 exit condition

- Hard-no check cleared (or pick adjusted)
- Broccoli bought, sampled, and in tonight's ritual

Then Day 6.

# Day 6 — Catch-up + the interview

> No new install. Five days of stacking gets a consolidation day — anything
> in flight gets closed out, anything shaky gets adjusted. Meanwhile the
> coach interviews. Every question below is quietly loading a future rung;
> none of that is disclosed.

## The day itself

- **Adjust and/or catch up.** Any Day 0–5 task still open closes today. Any
  friction (a meat that's stalling, a ritual step that keeps slipping) gets
  talked through and fixed. Nothing new lands.

## The interview (coach asks, in chat — conversational, not a form)

**1. Eating window:**

> "So far, about what time is the **earliest** you start eating? And the
> **latest**?"

- *Coach-side: this maps his current eating window. The eat-first rule
  already owns the front of it; the answer tells you where the back of the
  window sits — raw material for a future rung that trims late eating.
  Both ends, asked neutrally, no judgment attached.*

**2. Carb preferences — send the menu, get picks:**

> "Down the road we'll slot in some carbs. Here's a list of healthy,
> minimally processed options — **not exhaustive**, tell me if your
> favorite's missing. Which would you actually eat?"

- **White or brown rice** (microwave cups count — 90 seconds, portioned)
- **Potatoes** — russet or gold, microwaveable in minutes
- **Sweet potatoes** — same deal
- **Oats / oatmeal** — plain, not the sugared packets
- **Quinoa** (microwave pouches exist)
- **Canned beans or lentils** — rinse and eat
- **Fruit** — bananas, apples, oranges; frozen berries count
- **Corn tortillas**

- *Coach-side: this is inventory-gathering for the carb install, whenever
  that rung fires. Same pattern as the protein menu: pre-screened list,
  his picks, ownership stays with him. No purchase, no task — preferences
  only.*

**3. Water audit:**

> "Accurate number, not the ideal one: how many bottles of water are you
> actually getting through in a day, morning included?"

- *Coach-side: verifies the 2L habit is really running and calibrates
  total daily intake. The "accurate, not ideal" framing matters — you're
  asking for data, not a performance. His bottle size is known from Day 1,
  so bottles × size does the math.*

**Coach notes:**

- Three questions, one sitting, kept conversational. This is the program's
  first data pull since Day 0 — and the first one measuring *him* rather
  than his shopping.
- Nothing revealed about why each question is asked. The eating-window
  answer in particular should not telegraph that late eating may get
  trimmed — that invites pre-emptive gaming of the answer.
- Answers get logged coach-side. They are the load-in for the next phase:
  carbs (what), eating window (when), water (whether the foundation holds).

## Day 6 exit condition

- All Day 0–5 tasks closed
- Three interview answers logged

# PHASE 2 — DYNAMIC ADJUSTMENT (Days 8–30)

> The format changes here. Week one was a script; from Day 8 the coach
> **thinks for the client** and pulls levers based on what his data and
> check-ins show. No more fixed day-by-day rungs — instead: seven levers,
> the order they fire in, and the reads that trigger them. The client never
> sees this machinery. He just receives one adjustment at a time, same as
> week one.

## The seven levers

### Lever 1 — Protein up

- Incrementally raise protein intake, two routes:
  - **Bigger portions** of his current foods (the Day 4 number moves up), or
  - **New options, week-one style**: pick from his top 3 / the menu, buy,
    adopt, portion into the ritual. Same pattern, no new teaching needed.
- Direction of travel: protein becomes the backbone of every eating day.
  It's also quiet PSMF prep (Lever 7) — the higher his comfortable protein
  baseline, the cheaper a PSMF day is later.

### Lever 2 — Carb math (coach-side)

- Calculate how many healthy-carb servings bring him to a **good caloric
  range** — enough to make the day satisfying and sustainable, sitting
  inside a deficit.
- The client experiences this as "add another rice cup at dinner," never as
  arithmetic. All math stays coach-side, per the base plan's
  never-reveal-the-numbers rule — that one ports to this timeline intact.

### Lever 3 — Calories down

- Once the plate is built (Levers 1–2 running), **incrementally decrease
  calories** to deepen the deficit. Carbs are the dial — protein and
  vegetables don't get cut.
- Increments small, one at a time, each held long enough to read his
  response before the next. The client hears "we're trimming the rice to
  one cup" — never a calorie number.

### Lever 4 — The check-in (standing, throughout)

- Regular pulse: **adherence, issues, and how he's feeling.** Tell him
  directly: **his feelings are important data.** The process — and the
  coach's job — is to make all of this **effortless for him, as it should
  be.** Friction reported = the program adjusts, not the client toughing
  it out.
- Coach-side: the check-in is also the gate on every other lever. Nothing
  escalates onto a client who's straining. Feelings are the instrument
  panel for Levers 3, 5, 6, and 7.

### Lever 5 — Push the floor back (toward regular fasting)

- The Day 7 floor time moves **back in 2-hour increments** — one push at a
  time, each held until it feels like nothing, then the next. Slowly walks
  him into regular fasting without ever naming a protocol.
- **Stay caffeinated through the fasting window: coffee, tea, sparkling
  water** — all fine before the floor, all appetite management.
- Composition note: every push automatically shortens the eating window
  and drags the eat-first stack (water → prepped containers) later into
  the day — the prepped food starts owning an even larger share of total
  intake, for free.

### Lever 6 — Steps (only when nutrition is running)

- **Gate: the nutritional elements are installed and stable.** Then, and
  only then, introduce **10–15k steps per day.**
- This is the program's only movement install — consistent with the base
  ethos (no gym in 30 days; walking is the deficit lever with near-zero
  adherence cost and no skill requirement).

### Lever 7 — PSMF tests → final week

- **Occasional single-day PSMF tests**: eat only the prepped protein +
  vegetables — the carb sits out for the day. Note that his ritual has
  been quietly assembling the PSMF food set since Day 5; a test day is
  subtraction from an existing routine, not a new protocol.
- Sequence: **1-day test → read response → 2-day test → read response.**
  Response = adherence held, feelings steady at check-in, no rebound
  blowout after.
- **The final week is a PSMF — if he's responsive.** Earned by the test
  results, not scheduled regardless. If tests showed strain, the final
  week stays a regular deficit and that is a fine outcome.

### Lever 8 — The weigh-in ask (soft, early in Phase 2)

- **See if he'll give a weekly weigh-in.** Framed as an ask, not a mandate —
  Day 0 deliberately skipped the scale, and that grace doesn't get revoked
  by decree.
- Pitch shape: "One number a week helps me steer this for you. Same
  conditions every time — morning, after bathroom, before food or water.
  You never have to like the number; it's for me, not a scorecard."
- **If yes:** log weekly, same conditions each time. This becomes the
  primary responsiveness read for Levers 3 and 7 (calorie trims and PSMF
  earn/hold decisions).
- **If no:** don't push. Steer on the secondary instruments — adherence,
  check-in feel, clothes fitting, energy. The program survives without the
  scale; it's just steering with one less gauge.

### Lever 9 — The simplification ("from now on, life is simple")

- **Timing gate: fires only once the plate is fully built** (Levers 1–2
  running — protein backbone + calculated carbs feeding him properly). You
  cannot ban food outside the plan until the plan is feeding him enough;
  before that, this rule would just create hunger with nowhere to go.
- **Send to client, plainly:**
  > "From now on, life is simple. **No alcohol, no drinks with calories,
  > no dessert, no food outside our plan.** And here's the fun part: **go
  > find every zero-calorie soda and drink you like — those are unlimited.
  > Enjoy them.**"
- **Coach notes:**
  - This is the program's total-lockdown moment — the black box of
    untracked eating closes in one move. Everything before it was
    addition and ordering; this is the single decree that converts the
    plan from "part of his day" to "his entire intake."
  - The zero-calorie hunt is load-bearing, not a consolation: it gives an
    active, immediate *yes* alongside the *no*, keeps the flavor habit's
    slot filled, and mirrors the buy-several-brands pattern he already
    knows from yogurt and broccoli.
  - Once this lever fires, Levers 2 and 3 get vastly sharper — with no
    black box, coach-side calorie math is close to exact.

## Sequencing (loose arc, coach adjusts freely)

| Window | Focus |
|---|---|
| ~Days 8–12 | Levers 1–2: build the full plate (protein up, carbs calculated in). First floor push (Lever 5). **Weigh-in ask (Lever 8) early here.** |
| ~Days 13–18 | **Simplification (Lever 9) fires once the plate is built.** Lever 3 begins: first calorie trims. Second floor push. Lever 6 fires if nutrition is stable — steps in. |
| ~Days 19–23 | First 1-day PSMF test; if clean, 2-day test a few days later. Deficit continues between tests. |
| ~Days 24–30 | Final week: PSMF if earned, deeper regular deficit if not. Day 30 close-out. |

## Phase 2 operating rules

1. **One lever per adjustment.** Never two changes at once — otherwise a
   bad read can't be attributed.
2. **The check-in gates everything.** A strained client gets consolidation,
   not escalation. (Week one's install-install-consolidate cadence
   continues; consolidation days are still real days.)
3. **Numbers stay coach-side.** Calories, deficits, the word "PSMF," the
   fasting endgame of the floor pushes — none of it is disclosed. He
   receives food-level instructions only.
4. **Protein and vegetables are never the cut.** Carbs are the calorie
   dial; the ritual's protein core only ever grows or holds.
5. **Swap-don't-force survives all phases.** Any food that stalls gets
   swapped within its category, at any point, without resetting anything.

# DAY 31+ — THE EXIT RAMP

> The program does not end on its most aggressive protocol. After the PSMF
> (whether the final week or the last test), the client **comes back to the
> previous routine** — the built plate he ran before the PSMF began.

**Send to client (end of Day 30 / end of the PSMF):**

- "The push is done. **We go back to exactly what you were doing before
  this week** — same ritual, same containers, same floor time, same
  drinks rules. Nothing new to learn; you already lived it."

**Coach notes:**

- The return is to the **pre-PSMF deficit routine**, not to his old life —
  ritual, eat-first, floor time, simplification rules all keep running.
  The PSMF was a temporary deepening inside a structure that never stopped.
- Expect the scale to bounce a few lbs as carbs and their water return.
  Tell him before it happens, in plain terms: "the scale will tick up a
  couple pounds this week — that's water coming back with the carbs, not
  fat. It's expected and it's fine." An unwarned bounce is how clients
  torch a real result.
- What happens after Day 31+ — maintenance, a second cut, transition into
  the long-game base plan — is a separate conversation, held then.
